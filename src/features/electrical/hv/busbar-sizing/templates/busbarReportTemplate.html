2.0 Continuous Current Carrying Capacity of {{conductorType}} Conductor

2.1 The Heat Balance Equation is :

I² RF + qs = qc + qr + qcond {{citationHeatBalanceEq}}
where I = Current Through the Conductor, A
R = Direct Current Resistance at the Operating Temperature
F = Skin Effect Co-efficient
qs = Solar Heat Gain
qc = Convective Heat Loss
qr = Radiation Heat Loss
qcond = Conductive Heat Loss
qcond = 0.00 IEEE 605-2023 (Pg No: 42) (As per Clause 8.2.6)

a. The DC Resistance R is given by following equation,

For Aluminum Alloys
R =
8.145 × 10⁻⁴ C' × Ac * [ 1 + 0.00403 × C' 61 * (T₂ − 20) ]
{{citationDcResistance}}
Where, C' = Conductivity as % IACS = {{conductivityPctIACS}} % IEEE 605 - 2023 (Pg No: 29) Table 2
Ac = Cross-sectional area, sq. inch = {{crossSectionAreaIn2}} in²
T₂ = Conductor temperature, deg c = {{conductorTempC}} °C
R = 3.43E-06 Ω / ft

b. Forced Convection Heat Loss qc is given by :

qc = 0.010 * D−0.4 * A * ΔT (IEEE - 605-2023 , Pg No: 264)
Where, A = Conductor Surface Area per unit Length = 12 x D x π = 249.757 in² /ft
D = Outer Diameter of the Aluminum Tube = {{outerDiameterIn}} in
ΔT = Difference in Temp. in ° C b/w Conductor Surface Temp. & Ambient Temp.
Tc = Conductor Temperature = {{conductorTempC}} °C
Ta = Ambient Temperature = {{ambientTempC}} °C
ΔT = Tc - Ta = 90 - 35.6 = 54.4 °C
qc = 0.010 * {{outerDiameterIn}}^-0.4 * 249.757 * 54.4 W/ft
qc = {{qc}} W/ft

c. Radiation Loss qr is given by :

qr = 36.9 * 10⁻¹² * ε * A * [(Tc + 273)⁴ − (Ta + 273)⁴] (IEEE - 605-2023, Pg No: 264)
Where, A = Conductor Surface Area per unit Length = 249.757 in² /ft IEEE 605-2023 (Pg No:155 Table B.8)
ε = Emissivity Coefficient of the Conductor Surface = {{emissivity}} (IEEE - 605-2023, Pg No: 287,Table I.2)
Ta = Ambient Temperature = {{ambientTempC}} °C
Tc = Conductor Temperature = {{conductorTempC}} °C
qr = 36.9 × 10⁻¹² × {{emissivity}} × 249.757 × [( {{conductorTempC}} + 273)⁴ − ( {{ambientTempC}} + 273)⁴]
qr = {{qr}} W/ft

d. The Solar Heat Gain qs is given by :

qs = ε' * Qs * A' * K * Sin (θ) (IEEE - 605-2023, Pg No: 265)
Where, A' = Effective Projected Area of Conductor by unit length = 79.500 in² /ft (IEEE - 605-2023, Pg No:156)
where sinζ=1 and A=D for cylinder
ε' = Solar Absorption Coefficient = {{solarAbsorption}} IEEE 605 - 2023 (Pg No:171 & 172) Table C.6 & C.7
(generally taken equal to ε used for radiation loss)
K = Heat Multiplying Factors for Altitudes <1500m = 1.0 (IEEE - 605-2023 Pg No: 157, Table B.11)
θ = Effective Angle of Incidence of Sun (IEEE - 605-2023, Pg No: 194)
θ = Cos⁻¹ [ CosHc * Cos(Zc - Z₁ ) ]
Degrees north latitude = {{latitudeDeg}} °
Hc = Altitude of Sun for Latitude at Noon Time = {{solarAltitudeDeg}} ° (IEEE - 605-2023 Pg No: 156) Table B.9
Zc = Azimuth of Sun for Latitude at Noon Time = {{solarAzimuthDeg}} °
Z₁ = Azimuth of Conductor Line (0 for N-S and 90 for E-W) = {{conductorAzimuthDeg}} °
For worst case, you can consider 90° or 270° orientation
θ = {{theta}} °
Qs = Intensity of Solar Radiation = 1037.1 W/m² (IEEE - 605-2023 Pg No-157 Table B.10)
(considered 90° solar altitude) = 0.669030137 W/in²
qs = {{solarAbsorption}} × 0.669030137 × 79.5 × 1 × Sin( {{theta}}° )
qs = {{qs}} W/ft
